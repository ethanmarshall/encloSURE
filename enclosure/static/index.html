Designing a box from scratch

They're the same numbers EncloSURE uses, so a box you draw this way will pass its Fit check. They're starting points: print the fit test (Parts tab, "Thread and snap test print") once, and adjust to what your printer does.

Start from the board

  • Export the board from KiCad as STEP with its 3D models (File > Export > STEP) and bring it into your CAD as a reference. Draw the box around the real board, not a rectangle.
  • Put the box's origin at the board's centre (or a mounting hole) so moving the board moves everything with it.
  • Find the tallest part and anything under the board (through-hole leads stick out 1.5 to 2 mm). Those set the inside height and the standoff height.
  • Mark which connectors face which wall before you draw any walls.

Walls, floors and corners

Wall2.0 mm (about four lines at 0.45 mm); 1.2 mm minimum; 2.5 to 3 mm past about 150 mm long, or add ribs
Floor and lid2.0 mm; keep them close to the wall thickness so the part cools evenly
Outside corner radius3 mm or more; it prints cleaner and doesn't lift
Inside corner radiusthe outside radius minus the wall (a Shell feature does this for you)
Bottom edgea 0.4 to 0.5 mm chamfer hides the first layer's squish ("elephant's foot")
Ribsabout 60% of the wall thick, no more than 3 times the wall tall

Fits and clearances (per side)

Lid lip into the walls0.2 mm: slides in snug
Press fit0.05 to 0.1 mm
Loose or sliding0.3 to 0.4 mm
Parts that move (hinges, a pin)0.3 mm
Printed threads0.2 mm each (bolt smaller, nut bigger)
Holesvertical holes print about 0.1 to 0.2 mm small on the diameter; add that, or drill them out

Fits depend on your printer, filament and slicer, so print a test piece before a big part.

Lids

  • Lip: 3 mm deep, 1.6 mm thick, inside the walls with the fit clearance. It locates the lid and stops light and dust getting in at the seam.
  • Screw lid: corner posts about 2.2 to 2.5 times the screw size across (8 mm for M3), tied into the corner. Counterbore the heads half the lid deep.
  • Snap lid: a 0.5 mm rib on the lip into a groove in the wall. Hinge lid: knuckles with 0.3 mm gaps, and a 1.75 mm filament pin.
  • Living hinge: a 0.4 to 0.5 mm strip, printed in PETG, PP or nylon (PLA cracks), flexed a few times while warm.
  • Leave the lid's lip and anything hanging from the lid clear of the board's tallest part: 2 mm or more.

  • The insert sizes are for CNC Kitchen's standard inserts; other makers differ, so use the datasheet for the ones you buy.
  • Insert hole depth: the insert's length plus 0.5 to 1 mm, so melted plastic has somewhere to go. Leave at least 1.5 to 2 mm of plastic round the insert.
  • Self-tapping: get at least 1.5 times the screw size of thread into the plastic (4.5 mm for M3). Screws that bottom out crack the boss.
  • Pick screw lengths so the screw stops short of the hole's bottom. EncloSURE checks this for you.
  • In SolidWorks' Hole Wizard or Onshape's Hole feature, draw an insert hole as a plain hole at the maker's diameter, not a tapped hole.

Holding the board

  • Standoffs 5 mm tall: through-hole leads and solder joints need 3 mm or more. Make them about 2.5 times the screw across (7.5 mm for M3), and keep that inside the board's keep-out round the hole.
  • A gusset or two at the foot of tall standoffs keeps them from snapping.
  • Gap from the board's edge to the walls: 0.5 to 1 mm, less on a connector edge so the connector reaches the outside.
  • Headroom over the tallest part: 2 mm or more below the lid's lip.
  • Watch the corners: a screw lid's corner posts sit where a big board's corners would be.

Connectors and openings

  • Opening: the connector's face plus 0.25 to 0.5 mm each side, or the panel cutout from its datasheet.
  • Height: standoff + board thickness + the connector's centre above the board. Draw it from the board model, not by eye.
  • Set the connector's face within about 1 mm of the outside. A USB-C plug's overmold has to reach the receptacle; thin the wall there (to 1.2 mm) if you have to.
  • Openings in a wall make the board drop in at an angle or slide sideways. Check that it can actually go in (EncloSURE's insertion check does).
  • Panel parts (glands, switches, jacks): use the maker's recommended hole, and leave room inside for the nut: about 1.5 times the hole across.

Buttons, LEDs and displays

  • Button caps: 0.2 to 0.3 mm clearance in their hole each side, a flange inside so they can't fall out, and a short travel stop.
  • LEDs: a light pipe (a tube down from the lid) stops light bleeding to neighbouring LEDs; a 0.6 to 1 mm thin spot in a light-coloured wall glows through without a hole.
  • Displays: an opening the size of the active area, with a recess behind it for the glass or a clear panel, glued from inside.

Printability

  • Overhangs up to 45° print without support; flat roofs bridge up to about 10 to 20 mm.
  • Horizontal round holes over about 5 mm: a teardrop or a flat top so the roof doesn't sag.
  • Print the base opening up and the lid top down: no supports and the show faces come out smooth.
  • Text: 5 mm tall or more, strokes 0.8 mm wide or more, 0.6 mm deep or raised.
  • Snap hooks and anything that flexes: print so the layers run along the bending part, not across it.

Snap fits

  • Strain at the hook's root: ε = 1.5 t y / L² (t thickness, y undercut, L length), the straight-beam formula from the Bayer snap-fit guide.
  • Keep ε under roughly 1.5% for PLA and 2.5% for PETG or ABS (rules of thumb for printed parts). A longer, thinner beam bends further for the same strain.
  • A beam 5 to 10 times as long as it is thick, with a small fillet at the root.
  • A 90° catch face holds for good; 30 to 45° lets it pull off.

Heat

  • PLA softens from about 55 to 60 °C; PETG about 80 °C; ABS and ASA about 100 °C. In a car or the sun, use PETG or ASA.
  • Vents low on one side and high on the other let warm air rise out (a chimney).
  • Vent slots 2 mm wide keep fingers and most tools out; under 1 mm keeps wires out too.
  • A fan: vents across the box from it, so air crosses the board on its way out.

Sealing

  • An O-ring cord in a groove in the lid: groove about 1.4 times the cord wide and 0.75 times it deep (about 25% squeeze).
  • Keep the screws outside the seal, close enough that the lid doesn't bow between them.
  • Every hole needs its own seal: an IP-rated gland or connector, or a gasket under the part.
  • IP ratings: a first digit of 2 keeps out fingers (12.5 mm), 3 tools (2.5 mm), 4 wires (1 mm); real numbers need the IEC 60529 tests.

Assembly

  • Work out the order first: board in, screws, cables, lid. Every screw needs a straight path for the driver.
  • Leave room for cables to bend (about 5 times their diameter) and add a strain relief where they leave the box.
  • Put all screws on one side if you can, and the same size everywhere.

In Onshape and SolidWorks

  • Drive the design from a few numbers: Onshape's Variable Studio (or #variables) and configurations; SolidWorks' Global Variables (Tools > Equations) and configurations. Wall, clearance and inside size should each live in one place.
  • Build order: sketch the inside from the board plus its clearances, extrude it, fillet the vertical edges, then Shell with the top face removed. Filleting before the shell keeps the wall thickness even round the corners.
  • Make the lid as its own part, or split one solid at the lid line (Split in SolidWorks, Split Part in Onshape), then add the lip.
  • Project the board's mounting holes onto the floor sketch (Convert Entities in SolidWorks, Use in Onshape) so the standoffs follow the board if it changes.
  • Check for clashes: SolidWorks' Interference Detection, or a Boolean intersect with the board in Onshape.
  • Export STEP for EncloSURE's Fit tab: upload your base, lid and the board's STEP, and it checks the fit for you.

Sheet metal and moulding

  • Sheet metal: flanges at least 4 times the thickness long; holes at least 2.5 times the thickness plus the bend radius from a bend; inside bend radius about the thickness. Your shop's bend table wins.
  • Injection moulding: even walls (ABS about 1.2 to 3.5 mm), 1° of draft on walls along the pull, bosses and ribs at 60% of the wall or less (sink marks), inside radii at least half the wall. Openings in side walls need side actions, which cost more.
