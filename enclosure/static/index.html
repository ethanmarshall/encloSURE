EncloSURE

An enclosure from a size and a few choices. Sizes are in the units chosen at the top, measured from the centre of the inside, with Z up. Files are always millimetres. The preview updates as you type.

Describe it in words

Name the board (a Raspberry Pi, an Arduino Uno, or a size like 80x50), the connectors and controls, a display or batteries, and where it lives. It reads keywords, and lists everything it assumed.

Start from a finished board

Upload KiCad's STEP export of the board (File > Export > STEP, with 3D models), or just its .kicad_pcb (heights then come from parts.json or typical ones, listed). The box is sized around it, with standoffs under the mounting holes and openings for the connectors at its edges, then checked with the board inside. Every setting below stays editable.

A second board stacked on it (a HAT or shield)

The gap is from the first board's top to the second's bottom (11 mm for a Raspberry Pi HAT on its usual spacers; check yours). The second board sits on the first; its parts count for room and openings, named like J1/top.

Size

2 mm walls suit most printed boxes. Corner radius is on the outside; 0 for square corners.

Lid

Not sure of the fit gap? Print a fit test: six plugs from 0.10 to 0.35 mm, labelled, to try in their pockets.

A groove in the lid's underside presses the cord onto a ledge inside the walls, about 25% squeeze. Use solid O-ring cord.

Board standoffs

The distance between your board's mounting hole centres, centred in the box.

Snap-in pins: a split peg with a barb goes up through each mounting hole and the board clicks down over them. Print them solid.

Holes and cutouts

Pick a part to cut its maker's recommended hole, or give your own size. On a wall, position is along the wall from its centre and up from the inside floor.

Boards standing on edge

Card guides: pairs of rails on the front and back walls hold a board standing across the box. Give the X position of each.

Display windows

An opening with a recess behind it, so a clear panel or a display's glass sits flush from the inside.

Board under the lid

Posts hanging from the lid for a display or button board behind it, at the hole positions from its datasheet. Pair them with a window.

Light pipes

Tubes from the lid down to just above each LED on the board, for a light pipe or clear rod. Give the LED positions.

Heat

What the electronics turn into heat. The report estimates how warm the air inside gets, with and without vents.

Batteries

A cradle on the floor with room at the ends for spring contacts. Cell sizes are the usual maximums; check yours.

Cables

Zip-tie anchors on the floor, and notches in the top of a wall that the lid closes over a cable (fit a grommet).

Mounting

0 means none. Keyholes go in the floor so the box hangs on two screws.

Battery door and cables

The door prints as its own part and screws on flush. Size the opening to your battery holder.

A hole in the wall for the cable and a clamp bar on two posts inside it (the bar prints as its own part).

Cost and summary

Prices each piece of hardware from DigiKey (its first in-stock match: check before ordering) and the plastic from the weight estimate.

Made another way

The same inside size and holes as bent sheet metal or laser-cut panels, as DXF files a shop can cut; or a check of this printed design against the rules for injection moulding.

Tolerances

Clearance: the gap every part and the board edge keep from the enclosure. Opening margin: room around a connector's face in its opening. Setback: how far a connector's face may sit behind the outside of the wall before a plug can't reach it; check your plug's datasheet.

Holes in an off-the-shelf box. You get the box with its holes as STEP, and a PDF with a full-size drilling template for each face.
