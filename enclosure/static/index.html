EncloSURE

An enclosure from a size and a few choices. Everything is in millimetres, measured from the centre of the inside, with Z up. The preview updates as you type.

Size

2 mm walls suit most printed boxes. Corner radius is on the outside; 0 for square corners.

Lid

A groove in the lid's underside presses the cord onto a ledge inside the walls, about 25% squeeze. Use solid O-ring cord.

Board standoffs

The distance between your board's mounting hole centres, centred in the box.

Holes and cutouts

Pick a part to cut its maker's recommended hole, or give your own size. On a wall, position is along the wall from its centre and up from the inside floor.

Mounting

0 means none. Keyholes go in the floor so the box hangs on two screws.

3D printing

Start a KiCad board that fits your enclosure, then check the finished board: standoffs, connectors lined up with their openings, parts clear of the lid and walls.

1The enclosure

Export both from the same assembly in Onshape or SolidWorks so they line up. Without the lid, part heights are checked to the top of the walls.

Uses the box on the Quick box tab, as it is now.

2Design the board

Open the KiCad board. It has the outline, a mounting hole on each standoff with a copper keepout for the screw head, the tallest part allowed (notes on User.1) and where each opening lands (User.2). Place connectors on those markers.

Tolerances

Clearance: the gap every part and the board edge keep from the enclosure. Opening margin: room around a connector's face in its opening. Setback: how far a connector's face may sit behind the outside of the wall before a plug can't reach it; check your plug's datasheet.

Holes in an off-the-shelf box. You get the box with its holes as STEP, and a PDF with a full-size drilling template for each face.

Your box

Download the maker's 3D model (STEP) of the box you bought; Hammond has one on each 1590 product page. It must stand with its opening up.
